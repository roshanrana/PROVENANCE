**Run id:** `attest-20260907T033118Z-b0ba9b9` · **Date:** 2026-09-07
**Card:** NVIDIA H100 80GB HBM3, compute capability 9.0, driver 580.126.09

This is the number the project was built to produce, and it comes with a
correction to the result published an hour earlier.

## The figure

> **Batch invariance costs 22.7% of output throughput**
> ratio 0.773, 95% CI [0.741, 0.808], n = 128 per arm

| metric | invariance on / off | 95% CI | direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| output throughput | **0.773×** | [0.741, 0.808] | higher is better |
| TTFT p50 | **1.295×** | [1.276, 1.310] | lower is better |
| TTFT p99 | **1.129×** | [1.103, 1.142] | lower is better |

So: roughly **a quarter of your throughput, and about 30% on median latency**,
to make a shared inference endpoint reproducible. Both intervals exclude 1.0
comfortably, so the effect is resolved rather than merely suggested.

Interesting on its own: the p99 penalty (1.129×) is **smaller** than the p50
(1.295×). Invariance costs proportionally less in the tail than at the median —
the fixed reduction order removes some of the variance along with the speed.

## The correction

`bench/results/stage2-h100-2026-09-07.md` reported, at 32 trials and 64 max
tokens, 6 distinct logprob vectors with invariance off and **1** with it on, and
concluded `INVARIANCE_FIXES_DIVERGENCE`.

At 128 trials and 128 max tokens, on the same card:

| `batch_invariant` | trials | distinct logprob vectors |
|---|---|---|
| `False` | 128 | **34** |
| `True` | 128 | **5** |

**Batch invariance does not eliminate divergence at this configuration. It
reduces it by about 85%.**

The earlier run was underpowered — 32 trials did not have enough samples to
observe a residual that appears at roughly 4 in 128. That is a real difference
between the runs, not a contradiction: both numbers are correct for the
conditions they were measured under, and the earlier writeup's own
"does not establish" section already said it was a presence result with no
confidence interval and one configuration. It is amended rather than deleted.

The honest headline is therefore **not** "the flag fixes it". It is:

> Batched inference at temperature 0 produced 34 distinct outputs in 128
> identical requests. `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1` cut that to 5, at a cost of 22.7%
> of throughput. It is a large mitigation, not a guarantee.

Which is a more useful thing to be able to tell a model-risk function than
either extreme, and it is consistent with vLLM documenting the feature as beta
and under active development.

Two engine processes over one run-id, because `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT` is read at
vLLM import time. `PASS_A_SKIPS=1` then `PASS_B_SKIPS=0`: pass A refused the
invariance-on cell against the invariance-off engine and pass B picked it up.
Latency is per-request wall clock from a monotonic clock; throughput is output
tokens over that duration.

## What this still does not establish

- **One model, one card, one concurrency, one output length.** The 22.7% is a
  measurement of this configuration, not a constant. It is entirely plausible
  that the penalty moves with batch size and sequence length, and this run
  cannot say which way.
- **Why 5 residual vectors remain.** Whether that is a gap in the invariant
  kernel coverage, something outside the kernels, or an artefact of this
  configuration is not established here and should not be guessed at in a
  writeup.
- **Nothing about prefix caching**, which is off in both arms — the interaction
  D-06 pins off and ADR-009 exists to decompose.
- **Nothing about SGLang.** A-03's caching × determinism 2×2 remains unrun, and
  it is the arm that would separate the cost of determinism from the cost of
  losing the cache.
